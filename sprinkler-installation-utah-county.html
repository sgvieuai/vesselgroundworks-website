What's included

What goes into a new system

Zone layout

Lawn, beds and trees on separate zones, with heads spaced so every part of the lawn gets water.

Valves & manifold

Valves grouped in boxes you can get to, so future repairs are quick.

Heads & rotors

Sprays for small areas and rotors for big lawns, matched so each zone waters evenly.

Drip for beds & trees

Water goes to the roots of your plants instead of onto rock and mulch.

Smart controller

Waters based on the weather, so you're not running sprinklers in the rain.

Ready for secondary water

A filter where it's needed, since unfiltered secondary water carries sand that clogs nozzles and valves.

Done in the right order

Sprinklers go in before the sod

On a new yard, the grade gets set first, then the sprinkler system, then sod, rock and plants on top. That way nothing gets torn up later to fix a line.

Questions

Common questions

How much does a new sprinkler system cost?

It depends on the size of the yard, how many zones it needs, and how much is lawn versus beds on drip. Estimates are free, and you get a clear price before any work starts.

Can you add zones to my existing system?

Yes. We can add zones, extend lines to a new area, or convert spray zones to drip. For problems with a system you already have, see sprinkler repair.

Should sprinklers go in before or after sod?

Before. The grade gets set first, then the sprinkler system, then sod and rock on top.

Do you install drip irrigation?

Yes. Drip for flower beds, shrubs and trees is part of most systems we install.

Will it work with secondary water?

Yes. Most Utah County yards water with secondary water. We add a filter where it's needed so sand doesn't clog nozzles and valves.

Is there a rebate for a smart controller?

Central Utah Water's program pays up to $100 per smart controller. Rules can change, so check Utah Water Savers before you buy.
