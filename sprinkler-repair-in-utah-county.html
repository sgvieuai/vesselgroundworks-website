Troubleshooting

Common sprinkler problems and what usually causes them

Many Utah County homes water with secondary water (pressurized irrigation). It isn't filtered like tap water, so sand and grit are a common cause of clogged nozzles and valves that won't close.

Water bubbling up or a soggy spot

Usually a cracked pipe or fitting, or a leaking valve. If the spot stays wet with the sprinklers off, the leak is probably on the main line, which is under pressure all the time. Shut off the water to the sprinkler system and text us a photo.

A zone won't turn on

Often a bad solenoid, a broken wire or a controller problem. If you can hear the valve click but no water comes out, the valve itself or the water supply is the more likely cause.

A zone won't shut off

Usually grit or a torn diaphragm holding the valve open. This one wastes a lot of water fast, so turn off the water to the system until it's fixed.

Low pressure or heads that barely pop up

A break somewhere on the zone, a partly closed valve, a clogged filter or nozzles, or too many heads on one zone.

Brown or dry spots

Clogged or mismatched nozzles, sunken or tilted heads, or heads blocked by grass and plants. Older systems often have spots the spacing never reached.

Water hitting the sidewalk or house

Heads that are misaligned, broken or the wrong spray pattern. Fixing overspray saves water and keeps it away from the foundation.
